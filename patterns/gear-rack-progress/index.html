Your chainring is worn. Here is how to tell, and how to change it.

Lim Kah Wai, 24 October 2026. Scroll inside this page: the pinion above drives the rack.

Most of the bikes that come into the shop on Jalan Bendahara with a skipping chain do not need a new derailleur. They need a new chain, and about one in four also needs a new chainring. The ring wears slowly, so nobody notices until the new chain starts to slip under load on the climb up to Tambun.

How a ring wears

Each tooth takes the load on the side that faces forward. Over 8,000 to 15,000 km the front face of every tooth is ground back, and the gap between teeth stretches from a neat U into a hook. A worn chain speeds this up, because its rollers no longer sit at the bottom of the gap. They ride up the face and push it out of shape.

Look at the ring from the side in good light. A healthy tooth is symmetrical. A worn one leans back, and the tip has a thin lip on one side. Mechanics call it a shark fin. If you can see it from a metre away, the ring is done.

Check the chain first

Put a chain checker on the lower run. On an 11 or 12 speed chain, change it at 0.5 percent stretch. On 10 speed and below, 0.75 percent is still fine. If the chain is past 1 percent, plan for a ring as well: a new chain on an old ring will usually skip within the first 200 km.

No checker? Measure 12 full links with a steel rule. New, they measure 304.8 mm. At 306.3 mm the chain has stretched 0.5 percent.

Count the teeth before you buy

The number is stamped on the ring, next to the bolt circle diameter: 50T and 110 BCD on most compact road cranks, 34T on the small ring. Buy the same count. A 50 to 25 gear moves the bike 2 wheel turns for every turn of the cranks, which on a 700 x 25c tyre is about 4.21 m. Change the ring and every gear on the bike changes with it.

Changing it

You need a 5 mm hex key, a chainring bolt tool to hold the back nut, and a torque wrench. Take the chain off the ring first. Undo the five bolts a little at a time, in a star pattern, so the ring does not bind.

Line the new ring up so the small pin on its outer face sits behind the crank arm. The pin stops a dropped chain from wedging between the crank and the frame. Grease the bolt threads and tighten them in a star to 8 to 10 Nm.

Shift through every gear on the stand before you ride. The front derailleur may need a quarter turn on its cable, because the new teeth are taller than the old ones.
